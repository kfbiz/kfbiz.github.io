Starting a Business in Finland

A practical roadmap for turning a business idea into a real business in Finland. Use it as a starting point, then check the current requirements for your specific situation.

Before registration

1. Start with the business idea

Be clear about what you will sell, who will buy it and why they would choose you. Think about your target customers, competitors, pricing and the resources you need to start.

Practical KF Biz tip:

Do not start with the registration form. Start by testing whether the business idea makes commercial sense.

2. Build a simple business plan

Write down your business concept, customers, products or services, sales approach, competitors, pricing, startup costs and expected income. A business plan can also be useful when applying for financing or startup support.

Keep the first version practical. You can improve it as you learn more about the market.

3. Choose a business form

For a person starting alone, common options include a private entrepreneur (toiminimi) and a limited liability company (osakeyhtiö). The right choice depends on factors such as risk, scale, ownership, taxation and how you plan to operate.

See our Toiminimi vs Oy guide for a focused comparison.

4. Register the business

A toiminimi entrepreneur can start business operations through the Finnish Tax Administration's MyTax service, while an osakeyhtiö is founded through the Business Information System (YTJ). Depending on your situation, you may also register in the Trade Register and the Tax Administration's registers.

Your exact registration route depends on your business form and circumstances, so check the current official instructions before submitting anything.

5. Set up the practical foundations

1

Business bank account

Keep business transactions organised and separate from personal spending. Check what your bank requires when opening the account.

2

Accounting and records

Choose how bookkeeping, receipts, invoices and financial records will be handled from the beginning.

3

Insurance and pension obligations

Check YEL and any mandatory insurance requirements for your situation and industry.

4

Permits and sector requirements

Some activities require permits, notifications or other obligations. Check these before you start trading.

6. Understand your tax obligations

Check whether you need to register for VAT, the Prepayment Register and, if you employ people, the Employer Register. VAT registration requirements depend on the business activity and turnover rules. The current Finnish small-business VAT threshold is €20,000 in a calendar year, with specific rules that should be checked against your situation.

Do not rely on an old checklist: tax rules can change.

7. Prepare for your first customer

Before launch, make sure you know how customers will contact you, how you will quote or invoice, what your payment terms are and how you will deliver the product or service.

Then start selling. Your first version does not need to be perfect; it needs to be clear enough for a customer to understand and buy.

Quick checklist

Ready to start?

  1. Business idea and target customer defined
  2. Basic market and competitor research completed
  3. Business plan prepared
  4. Business form chosen
  5. Registration route checked
  6. Tax registrations and obligations checked
  7. Banking and bookkeeping arranged
  8. Insurance, YEL and permits checked
  9. Pricing and sales process prepared
  10. First-customer plan ready
